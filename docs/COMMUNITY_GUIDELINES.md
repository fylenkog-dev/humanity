# HUMANITY — Community guidelines and moderation framework

_Status: proposed policy for review; not legal advice and not yet an operating moderation service._

## Our purpose

HUMANITY is intended to make room for honest personal stories and respectful encounters across different backgrounds. Disagreement and difficult experiences may be shared; harassment and dehumanization are not acceptable.

## Core expectations

### 1. Treat people with dignity
- Critique ideas and actions without attacking a person's humanity.
- No slurs, dehumanizing language, targeted harassment, or celebration of violence against people.
- Do not organize dogpiles or encourage others to target a person.

### 2. Protect privacy
- Do not publish another person's private contact details, exact location, private messages, identity documents, or other sensitive personal information without a valid, clear basis and consent.
- Do not use HUMANITY to identify, track, threaten, or expose someone.
- Authors should avoid unnecessary identifying details about themselves and others.

### 3. Be honest about stories
- Share first-hand experiences as personal accounts, not as proof of every claim about a group.
- Do not impersonate another person or fabricate a story presented as a real testimony.
- Clearly label fiction, composites, translations, and substantial reconstructions.
- Do not misrepresent edits or remove context in a way that changes the author's meaning.

### 4. No threats or encouragement of harm
- Threats, incitement, instructions to harm people, and praise intended to encourage imminent violence are not allowed.
- Content indicating immediate danger requires a defined escalation process; the platform must not imply it provides emergency response.

### 5. No exploitation or abuse
- No sexual exploitation, grooming, coercion, or exploitative content involving minors.
- No non-consensual intimate imagery.
- No content that facilitates trafficking, abuse, or exploitation.

### 6. No spam or manipulation
- No repetitive unsolicited promotion, scams, fraudulent fundraising, deceptive links, or coordinated manipulation.
- Commercial or sponsored content must not be disguised as a personal story.

## Sensitive stories and difficult topics

HUMANITY should not exclude a story simply because it discusses grief, discrimination, conflict, illness, trauma, or another difficult experience. Reviewers should consider context, necessity, consent, and risk.

Where appropriate:
- Add a content note so readers can make an informed choice.
- Remove unnecessary graphic detail while preserving the author's meaning.
- Avoid diagnosing people or presenting unverified allegations as established fact.
- Give authors control over whether they want identifying details included.

## Moderation principles

1. **Context matters.** Review the whole contribution and its context, not isolated words alone.
2. **Proportionate action.** Use the least restrictive action that adequately addresses the risk.
3. **Consistency.** Apply the same standards regardless of the author's nationality, language, viewpoint, or popularity.
4. **Explain decisions.** Tell the author what rule was involved and what can be changed, unless doing so would create a safety risk.
5. **Human review for serious decisions.** Automated tools may help prioritize queues but should not be the sole basis for high-impact decisions.
6. **Protect reporters.** Do not reveal a reporter's identity to the reported person.
7. **Allow appeals.** Provide a meaningful way to request a second review.

## Proposed moderation outcomes

- **Approve:** content meets the guidelines.
- **Approve with content note:** story is allowed but readers should receive context.
- **Request edits:** a specific, fixable issue needs the author's attention.
- **Restrict distribution:** temporarily limit visibility while a credible safety concern is reviewed.
- **Remove:** content violates a core rule or creates unacceptable risk.
- **Suspend or restrict an account:** repeated or severe abuse, subject to review and appeal.
- **Escalate:** credible threats, exploitation, or urgent safety issues require the designated specialist process.

These are proposed workflow states, not features currently available.

## Reporting and appeals

A future report flow should:
- Let readers identify the specific content and select a reason.
- Accept a short optional explanation.
- Avoid asking reporters for unnecessary personal information.
- Confirm receipt without promising a response time until staffing is defined.
- Keep reports confidential to the extent operationally possible.
- Provide authors with a reason and appeal route when content is restricted or removed.

## Reviewer checklist

Before deciding, reviewers should ask:
1. What is the specific policy concern?
2. Is the content first-person experience, opinion, allegation, or instruction?
3. Is a person identifiable, and could publication expose them to harm?
4. Is the risk immediate, credible, and specific?
5. Can a content note, redaction, or author edit resolve the issue?
6. Is the decision consistent with comparable cases?
7. What explanation and appeal path should be provided?

## Operational requirements before launch

A written policy is not a moderation service. Before public submissions, HUMANITY needs:
- Named policy ownership and trained reviewers.
- Queue access controls and reviewer confidentiality.
- Documented escalation contacts and incident handling.
- A process for appeals and repeat-offender decisions.
- Clear response expectations based on actual staffing.
- A privacy and retention schedule for reports and moderation records.
- A process to handle urgent reports without implying 24/7 coverage unless it exists.

## Current limitation

The repository contains a static prototype only. There is no reporting, moderation queue, account restriction, appeal mechanism, or emergency response operation. These guidelines are planning material and must not be presented to visitors as an active enforcement system until those capabilities exist and are staffed.
